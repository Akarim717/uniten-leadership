01 / The case for change

From a leadership bootcamp to a leadership ecosystem.

The inaugural bootcamp on 30 October–1 November 2026 is a promising starting point. The opportunity is to move beyond participation in training and establish a coherent way to define, develop, demonstrate and sustain leadership capability.

Academic leadership now requires navigating competing expectations across educational quality, people, institutional sustainability, digital disruption, industry relevance and long-term strategy. UNITEN needs a shared leadership language grounded in observable behaviour and real institutional responsibilities.

What does excellent leadership at UNITEN look like, and how will we recognise and develop it?

03 / Expectations by role

One framework. Different leadership demands.

The proposal separates the scale of responsibility from demonstrated proficiency. A leadership title is not, by itself, evidence of proficiency.

Three leadership levels

Level A • Leading Self & Teams

Heads of Department; Programme Leaders; emerging leaders

From individual academic excellence to leading others and small teams.

Level B • Leading Academic Units

Deputy Deans; Deans; Directors and equivalents

From team delivery to integrated performance of a college or substantial function.

Level C • Leading the Enterprise

VC; DVCs and comparable institutional executives

From unit optimisation to enterprise-wide choices, culture and ecosystem leadership.

Four proficiency stages

1 • Developing

Recognises the capability and applies it with guidance in relatively contained contexts.

2 • Effective

Applies it consistently and independently within defined responsibilities.

3 • Advanced

Handles ambiguity, conflicting objectives and complex stakeholders; supports others.

4 • Transformational

Shapes enterprise practice, changes systems and builds sustainable capability beyond own role.

01Executive Summary⌄

UNITEN is convening its first leadership bootcamp for senior and middle academic leaders under the theme “Leading the Energy University of the Future”. The three-day programme addresses institutional strategy, execution, resource stewardship, digital leadership, SDG/ESG, leadership ecosystem building, people and politics, and personal leadership planning. It creates a useful common starting point. However, a bootcamp by itself cannot establish a reliable and renewable leadership talent pool.

This proposal recommends establishing the UNITEN Leadership Competency Framework (ULCF) as the institutional reference for defining leadership expectations, diagnosing development needs, designing a continuous leadership programme, recognising demonstrated capability, and informing fair and evidence-based succession planning. The proposed architecture includes eight competency domains, three role levels, four proficiency stages, behaviour-based assessment, action learning, mentoring/coaching and periodic talent reviews.

The framework should be co-designed, validated and piloted before adoption. Decisions on formal talent classification, appointment and succession will remain subject to existing UNITEN policies, appropriate governance and human judgement.

021. Strategic Rationale⌄

Academic leadership is no longer principally a matter of administration or academic seniority. Leaders must reconcile academic quality, financial sustainability, staff capability, learner needs, industry relevance, digital disruption, research aspirations, and stakeholder expectations. UNITEN’s Energy University positioning requires leaders who can translate institutional ambition into choices and coordinated action across colleges and functions.

The strategic leadership literature distinguishes knowing institutional strategy from anticipating change, interpreting complex situations, making trade-offs, mobilising people and learning from results. The supplied Harvard Business Review guide identifies anticipation, challenge, interpretation, decision, alignment and learning as complementary strategic leadership capabilities. Watkins’ Six Disciplines adds pattern recognition, systems analysis, mental agility, structured problem-solving, visioning and political savvy. Olson and Simerson distinguish gaining strategic insight from driving strategic change. These sources inform the proposed framework but do not substitute for local validation.

A sustained approach will help UNITEN move from attendance-based leadership development to evidence of leadership capability and readiness. It will also support continuity when office-holders change, make development expectations clearer and create more deliberate opportunities for emerging talent.

032. Purpose and Intended Outcomes⌄

The purpose of ULCF is to articulate what effective leadership looks like at UNITEN and to connect that definition to professional development, institutional priorities and leadership continuity.

  • Establish a shared language of leadership across academic units and senior management.
  • Define observable behaviours and differentiated standards for emerging, unit-level and enterprise-level leaders.
  • Identify strengths and development needs through proportionate, multi-source evidence.
  • Build a coherent, continuous leadership pathway linking learning, practical assignments, coaching and reflection.
  • Create a transparent, developmental leadership talent pool and strengthen succession readiness.
  • Evaluate whether leadership development contributes to institutional outcomes, while avoiding simplistic attribution.
043. Scope and Design Principles⌄

Initial scope: Vice-Chancellor/Deputy Vice-Chancellors, Deans, Deputy Deans and comparable academic leadership positions, with a subsequent pathway for Heads of Departments, Programme Leaders and emerging academic leaders. The role taxonomy should be confirmed during design to avoid excluding equivalent positions or conflating job title with leadership scope.

  • Institution-specific and strategy-led, rather than a copied generic competency catalogue.
  • Behavioural, observable and assessable, with performance evidence not merely self-declaration.
  • Common leadership principles with expectations calibrated to level and decision authority.
  • Developmental first, with confidentiality, fairness, opportunity and feedback built in.
  • Inclusive of diverse disciplinary and career pathways, and sensitive to academic collegiality.
  • Practical and selective: no more domains than leaders and reviewers can meaningfully use.
  • Reviewed regularly as strategy, technology, sector conditions and roles evolve.
054. Proposed UNITEN Leadership Competency Model⌄

The eight domains below are a proposed starting architecture. Each requires behavioural validation through interviews, job analysis, critical incident examples and input from academic and professional-services stakeholders.

DomainWorking definition
1. Strategic & Futures LeadershipAnticipates trends, scans the higher education and energy landscape, thinks systemically, frames choices and translates the UNITEN mission into priorities.
2. Execution & Results LeadershipSets priorities, aligns plans and resources, makes trade-offs, establishes accountability, monitors outcomes and adapts execution.
3. People & Talent LeadershipBuilds productive teams, coaches colleagues, addresses performance issues, supports wellbeing and grows successors.
4. Change & Innovation LeadershipLeads purposeful organisational change; uses evidence, experimentation, AI and digital possibilities responsibly to improve practice.
5. Academic & Enterprise StewardshipSafeguards academic standards and integrity while exercising financial, operational, risk and governance judgement.
6. Influence & Stakeholder LeadershipBuilds trust and alliances, communicates convincingly, negotiates differences and works across institutional and external boundaries.
7. Responsible & Sustainable LeadershipMakes ethically sound and inclusive decisions and integrates sustainability, societal contribution and long-term value into institutional choices.
8. Personal Leadership & Learning AgilityDemonstrates self-awareness, credibility, courage, resilience, receptiveness to feedback and ongoing reflective learning.

Cross-cutting expectation: every domain should be enacted with integrity, attention to student learning and academic quality, and sensitivity to the consequences of AI use. The framework must distinguish genuine leadership behaviour from enthusiasm for a tool, technology or slogan.

098. Assessment, Evidence and Leadership Passport⌄

Assessment should combine multiple forms of evidence rather than use a single score or personality instrument as a proxy for leadership potential. Recommended inputs are self-reflection, manager/peer feedback, proportionate 360-degree observations, facilitated simulation performance, direct workplace evidence and action-learning portfolios.

The proposed UNITEN Leadership Passport would document: role and aspiration; competency-based strengths and development priorities; agreed individual development plan; selected stretch experiences; evidence of decisions and results; coaching reflections; and periodic review of growth. Sensitive feedback should be access-controlled and used only for declared purposes.

Assessment standards must include assessor guidance, evidence thresholds, moderation, confidentiality, bias checks, a right to factual correction, and a separation between developmental coaching notes and formal talent review information. Formal use for appointments or employment consequences should require an approved policy and a separate governance decision.

109. Leadership Talent Pool and Succession⌄

Participation or attendance should not imply talent-pool status or future appointment. The proposed talent process should distinguish demonstrated performance, future potential, role interest, mobility/availability and readiness. A moderated panel may consider readiness categories such as Ready Now, Ready in 1–2 Years, Ready in 3–5 Years and High Potential / Further Experience Required. These labels are provisional, not automatic entitlements or appointment decisions.

Each key leadership position should have a role success profile, critical experience requirements, foreseeable succession risks and a development slate. Talent review should incorporate diverse evidence and transparent criteria, without treating incumbents’ personal preferences or visibility as sufficient proof of capability.

1312. Resourcing and Implementation Risks⌄

A costed plan should follow scoping. Likely resource categories include project coordination, framework design and validation, assessment tools, trained facilitators/coaches, digital portfolio support, release time for participants and evaluation. Wherever possible, build on existing institutional capacity and use external expertise selectively for assessment validation or coaching.

RiskSuggested mitigation
Framework becomes a tick-box exerciseLimit domains; anchor descriptors to real leadership episodes and work outcomes.
Perceived bias or politicisation of talent decisionsUse multiple assessors, transparent criteria, moderation and an appeals/correction process.

1413. Decisions Requested from Top Management⌄
  • Approve in principle the establishment of the UNITEN Leadership Competency Framework and continuous leadership development architecture.
  • Appoint an executive sponsor and designate a lead owner for a cross-functional design team.
  • Authorise a 12-month phased consultation, design, pilot and evaluation process, with detailed workplan and budget returned for approval.
  • Endorse the eight-domain model and three role levels as a consultation draft, not as final policy.
  • Approve the bootcamp as the launch platform and request an initial leadership challenge and development commitment from each participant.
  • Require a management review at pilot completion before formal integration with appointment, appraisal or succession procedures.
1514. Closing Recommendation⌄

The inaugural bootcamp is the beginning of UNITEN’s opportunity to build leadership continuity. The strategic question is not simply who attends the next course, but which capabilities UNITEN must cultivate now to lead the Energy University of the Future. A calibrated competency framework, coupled with authentic leadership experiences and fair evidence-based review, can turn a successful event into a durable institutional asset.
